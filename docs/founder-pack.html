Nextup lets people find unsigned and rising artists early and take a real stake in them — either by owning a specific song outright at a flat price, or by taking a position on an artist's live, demand-driven price. Everything settles in cryptocurrency. There are no subscriptions and no tiers.

The honest headline: the product is substantially built and nothing is live.

Sixteen pages, nineteen database tables, and the full money path all exist and work. But the site is not hosted anywhere, no payment credentials are configured, and not one person has ever signed in. Four things stand between here and a real launch, and three of them need a human, not more code.

Between here and launch

Four blockers.

Only one of these is a coding problem.

  • Coinbase accountA real Coinbase Commerce business account has to be created and its two secrets configured. Until then the payment functions deliberately return "crypto payments aren't configured yet" rather than pretending to work. Needs the founder.
  • Function deploymentSeven edge functions are written and committed but never deployed — the deploy step has needed a manual approval that hasn't been granted in any working session. Needs one approval.
  • Legal reviewThe three policy pages are agent-written drafts. They should not be relied on by a real user in their current state. Needs a lawyer.
  • HostingThe domain nextup.exchange is owned but points nowhere. The build is a static bundle and can go up quickly once someone says go. Needs a decision.
Worth knowing

Decisions already taken.

These shaped the product and are not obvious from looking at it.

  • No subscriptionsA tiered monthly-support model was built, then removed on the founder's call. Backing is a live price plus wallet deposits and withdrawals — that is the only mechanism, by design.
  • Trading is switched onThe bonding-curve product sits behind a feature flag that ships off. It is currently on, at the founder's explicit instruction, with the founder owning the licensing and jurisdiction question.
  • Crypto onlyNo cards, no bank transfers, anywhere in the system.
  • Withdrawals are manualCoinbase Commerce can accept crypto but cannot send it. A withdrawal debits the wallet immediately and a person sends the funds. This is documented rather than hidden, and it stays true until a payout provider is integrated.
  • Admins are made in SQLThere is deliberately no UI for granting the admin role. A console that can mint admins is an escalation surface, and with one operator it would be all risk.
  • Famous names are placeholdersThe preview shows Bruno Mars, Justin Bieber, The Weeknd, Rihanna and Shakira with their real public listener figures, so the design can be judged populated instead of empty. None of them has agreed to anything. The live database still holds five fictional seed artists.
How money is protected

The one rule.

The browser is never trusted with who you are, what you hold, or what anything costs. Every action that moves money runs through a server-side function that derives your identity from a verified token and does the arithmetic itself. Every table has row-level security on from the moment it is created; a signed-in user can read their own rows and nobody else's.

Two real holes were found and closed this way. A leftover policy would have let any signed-in user grant themselves any unowned song for free. And two separate mistakes in locking down privileged database functions — opposite errors with the same root cause — would each have exposed them publicly. Both were caught by running the security advisor after every change, which is now routine.
