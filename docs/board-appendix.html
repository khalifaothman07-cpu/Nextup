01

Summary.

NextUp has completed a substantial pre-launch technical foundation covering artist discovery, track commerce, wallet accounting, bonding-curve transactions, access-control architecture and activity-based artist momentum rankings. The platform has not yet launched publicly. The remaining work is concentrated around deployment, regulated-market review, payment-provider activation, operational administration, artist infrastructure and independent security testing.

This appendix describes what exists, what does not, and what the board is being asked to decide. It is written to be read alongside the commercial proposal rather than in place of it: the technical position is materially further advanced than the commercial one, and Section 09 sets out the areas where the company still needs to define its model rather than its architecture.

02

The product.

NextUp is a two-sided platform. Listeners discover unsigned and rising artists early and take a position in their progress; artists gain a direct revenue relationship with the people who found them first, without a label intermediating it.

Listeners have two ways to participate:

  • Own the Song A one-time purchase of a specific track at a flat, artist-set price. One holder per track. The precise nature of what this conveys is an open legal question — see Section 09.
  • Back the Artist A continuously priced position in an artist, funded from a platform wallet, where price responds to real demand. This is the higher-value and higher-risk half of the product and is treated throughout as a regulated-market question rather than a purely technical one.

Artists receive a profile, a catalogue, a revenue relationship, and an analytics dashboard reporting genuine platform activity. Ranking is driven by an activity-based momentum score computed from real platform behaviour only — follows, transactions and purchases. It contains no editorial weighting and no imported or estimated figures, and the calculation is shown to users in full.

All settlement is in cryptocurrency. There are no subscriptions and no tiers.

04

Technology.

  • Application A modern JavaScript single-page application, statically built and deployable to any standard hosting provider. No proprietary or vendor-locked runtime.
  • Platform A secured Supabase production environment providing the database, authentication and server-side functions. A managed, industry-standard platform with an established security posture.
  • Payments Cryptocurrency settlement through a commercial payment processor. The company does not custody payment credentials and never handles card or bank details.
  • Source control A private company GitHub repository. Full change history, with the reasoning for each significant decision recorded alongside the change.
  • Documentation Architecture, data model, security posture, deployment procedure and a complete decision log are maintained in-repository and current.
05

Security architecture.

The governing principle: the user's browser is never trusted with identity, balance or price.

Every action that moves value is executed server-side. The user's device states an intention; it never states who it is, what it holds, or what something costs. Identity is derived from a verified session on the server, and all monetary arithmetic is performed there.

Data access is enforced at the database layer rather than in the application, so a user can only ever retrieve their own financial records regardless of how the request is made. Administrative capability is separately gated and every administrative action is written to an append-only record that administrators themselves cannot alter or remove.

Assurance to date, stated plainly. Security has been verified through automated platform advisory tooling run after every structural change, targeted testing of access boundaries, and a documented review of every permission in the system. Issues identified during development were remediated and recorded. The platform has not undergone independent third-party security testing. Commissioning that review is a prerequisite to public launch and is included in the funding requirement.

06

Treasury and customer funds.

This is called out separately because it is the most consequential outstanding item and it is architectural, not cosmetic.

The platform currently models customer balances only. There is no representation of company-held liquidity, no formal separation between company funds and customer funds, and no double-entry ledger reconciling what the company holds against what it owes.

Any company capital committed as operating liquidity must be modelled as a distinct, company-owned account that is structurally incapable of being commingled with customer balances, with continuous reconciliation between obligations and holdings. This affects solvency reporting, audit, regulatory classification and the ability to demonstrate that customer funds are intact at any moment.

Recommendation: this work should precede any real customer money entering the system, and should be specified with input from counsel and an accountant rather than designed engineering-first. It is placed accordingly in the roadmap.

07

Roadmap.

Sequenced by dependency rather than ambition. Legal definition precedes financial architecture, which precedes controls, which precede exposure to real customers. Durations assume a funded team and are indicative.

Phase 1 · Months 0–3 · Containment

Establish what the product legally is.

  1. Legal product-definition review across target jurisdictions
  2. Define precisely what "Own the Song" transfers — copyright, master rights, licence, collectable status, resale rights or royalty participation
  3. Treasury, custody and customer-funds architecture
  4. Automated financial and access-control regression testing, and completion of remaining launch controls
Phase 2 · Months 3–6 · Controlled exposure

Operate the system with real people, at small scale.

  1. Administrative and jurisdiction-control interface
  2. Independent third-party security assessment
  3. Private sandbox deployment
  4. Secure the first ten artists under contract
  5. Closed beta with a capped participant group and capped exposure
Phase 3 · Months 6–12 · Operating capability

Make it viable to run, not just to use.

  1. Automated treasury and withdrawal processing, replacing operator handling
  2. Complete artist-operations and permissions interfaces
  3. Artist acquisition pipeline — contracting, onboarding and support
  4. Financial reporting, reconciliation and audit readiness
Phase 4 · Months 12–18 · Scale

Grow only once the transaction model is proven.

  1. Public launch in cleared jurisdictions
  2. Community and marketplace functionality
  3. Visual and brand development
  4. Institutional and label partnership channels

Capital requirement.

The technical position can be stated with confidence. The financial position cannot yet, and this appendix does not assert figures it cannot support. The categories below are the ones the funding case must cover; the amounts require the company's own commercial modelling and are not estimated here.

To be completed before the board proposal is final

  • Legal and regulatory — product-definition review, jurisdictional opinions, entity and licensing structure, ongoing counsel
  • Independent security assessment — third-party testing and remediation
  • Operating liquidity — company capital committed as working float, held separately from customer funds per Section 06
  • Engineering — team cost across the four roadmap phases
  • Artist acquisition — advances, guarantees or incentives required to secure the first cohort under contract
  • Unit economics — customer acquisition cost, platform take rate, artist revenue share, expected transaction volume, retention, reserve requirements and resulting runway
  • Operations — infrastructure, payment processing costs, compliance and support headcount

Of these, unit economics is the single largest gap in the proposal. The platform's technical behaviour is well understood; its commercial behaviour is not yet modelled, and no figure in this appendix should be read as implying otherwise.

09

Decisions requested.

Each of these is outside an engineering mandate and is put to the board directly.

Decision 1

Regulatory classification of the Backing product

The continuously priced Backing mechanic is the platform's most valuable feature and its largest single risk. Depending on jurisdiction and final structure it may attract securities, derivatives, custody, gaming or virtual-asset regulation. The board is asked to authorise a formal legal determination, and to decide whether the platform launches with Backing enabled, restricted to cleared territories, or withheld pending licensing.

Decision 2

What "Own the Song" actually conveys

The purchase is currently described as permanent ownership of a track. What that transfers is not yet legally defined — whether copyright, master rights, a licence, collectable status, resale rights or royalty participation. This determines the product's consumer proposition, its tax treatment, its accounting treatment and its regulatory exposure. It requires resolution before any real purchase is accepted.

Decision 3

Treasury structure and custody model

Whether the company holds customer funds directly, engages a regulated custodian, or restructures so that it never takes custody at all. This decision drives Section 06's architecture, the licensing position, and the credibility of the operating-liquidity commitment.

Decision 4

Payment and payout infrastructure

The current processor accepts inbound payment but cannot send funds out, which is why withdrawals are operator-processed. A full payout provider is required before scale. The board is asked to approve selection and the associated compliance onboarding.

Decision 5

Artist acquisition strategy and commitment

Artists are the product, and there is not yet a contractual, operational or financial pipeline for securing and supporting them. The board is asked to approve the commercial terms offered to the first cohort and the budget behind them.

Decision 6

Launch jurisdiction and sequencing

Which territories the platform opens in, in what order, and on what regulatory basis. This determines what jurisdictional enforcement must do before it can be built.

10

Position.

The engineering work demonstrates that NextUp is real and technically substantial. It does not, on its own, demonstrate that the business is legally containable, commercially executable or financially controlled — and this appendix is deliberate in not implying otherwise.

The three items that most need resolution are not engineering problems. They are the regulatory classification of the Backing product, the legal definition of what a song purchase conveys, and a treasury structure that separates company liquidity from customer funds. Each is put to the board in Section 09.
